# Top 20 Chart Patterns

Reference for reading a chart: what each pattern looks like, which way it leans, what confirms it, the measured target, and where it fails.

## How to read a chart

1. Name the trend first: higher highs and higher lows is an uptrend, lower highs and lower lows is a downtrend, overlapping swings is a range.
2. Look for the patterns below.
3. A pattern is only confirmed on a close through its trigger line. Before that, call it "forming".
4. State the trigger, the measured target and the invalidation level, and check volume.

## Reversal patterns

1. **Head and Shoulders** (bearish, at a top): left shoulder, higher head, lower right shoulder; neckline through the two troughs. Trigger: close below neckline, volume often fades on the right shoulder. Target: head-to-neckline height projected down. Invalid: close above right shoulder high.
2. **Inverse Head and Shoulders** (bullish, at a bottom): mirror of #1. Trigger: close above neckline on rising volume. Target: height projected up. Invalid: below right shoulder low.
3. **Double Top** ("M", bearish): two highs near the same level with a trough between. Trigger: close below the trough. Target: top-to-trough height down. Invalid: new high above both tops.
4. **Double Bottom** ("W", bullish): two lows near the same level. Trigger: close above the middle peak. Target: height projected up. Invalid: new low below both.
5. **Triple Top** (bearish): three tests of resistance; trigger break of the support under them. Target: pattern height.
6. **Triple Bottom** (bullish): three tests of support; trigger break of resistance above them. Target: pattern height.
7. **Rounding Bottom / Saucer** (bullish): slow U-shaped base over weeks/months, volume dries up at the low. Trigger: break of the rim (prior high). Target: depth projected up.
8. **Rising Wedge** (bearish): price climbs inside converging upward-sloping lines, momentum fading. Trigger: close below lower line. Target: back to wedge start.
9. **Falling Wedge** (bullish): price falls inside converging downward-sloping lines. Trigger: close above upper line. Target: back to wedge start.
10. **V-Top / V-Bottom (spike reversal)**: sharp move reverses with no base, often on news/capitulation volume. Confirmed only by follow-through; hard to trade, avoid chasing.

## Continuation patterns

11. **Bull Flag**: strong pole up, then a short downward-sloping or flat channel on light volume. Trigger: break above flag top. Target: pole length added to breakout. Invalid: flag retraces more than ~50% of pole.
12. **Bear Flag**: pole down, then a small upward drifting channel. Trigger: break below flag. Target: pole length down.
13. **Bull Pennant / Bear Pennant**: like flags, but the pause is a small symmetrical triangle. Same trigger/target logic as flags.
14. **Ascending Triangle** (usually bullish): flat resistance with rising lows. Trigger: close above resistance. Target: triangle's widest height added. Invalid: break of rising trendline.
15. **Descending Triangle** (usually bearish): flat support with falling highs. Trigger: close below support. Target: widest height down.
16. **Symmetrical Triangle** (neutral, tends to continue the prior trend): lower highs and higher lows converge. Trade the break direction; target = widest height. Breakouts late in the apex (>75%) are weaker.
17. **Cup and Handle** (bullish): rounded U "cup", then a small pullback "handle" in the upper half of the cup on light volume. Trigger: break above the handle/rim. Target: cup depth added.
18. **Rectangle / Trading Range**: horizontal support and resistance with several touches each. Buy support/sell resistance inside; trade the breakout with target = range height.
19. **Channel (ascending / descending)**: parallel trendlines. Trend continues while inside; a break of the lower line of an up channel (or upper of a down channel) warns of reversal.

## Gaps

20. **Gaps**: *breakaway* gap out of a base on high volume (start of trend, often doesn't fill); *runaway/measuring* gap mid-trend (often marks halfway); *exhaustion* gap late in a trend followed by reversal (often fills fast); *common* gap inside a range (usually fills). An island reversal is an exhaustion gap followed by a gap the other way.

## Checks for every read

- Volume should expand on the breakout; a low-volume break is suspect (possible fakeout).
- A failed pattern is itself a signal: a bull flag that breaks down or a head-and-shoulders that reclaims the neckline often runs hard the other way.
- Confluence: tracked key levels (PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, VIX zones) near a trigger strengthen it.
- Higher timeframe trend outranks the lower timeframe pattern.
- These are educational reads of price action, not trade recommendations.
